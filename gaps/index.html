The open questions

The open questions, G01–G11

The structural questions the thesis raises and its laboratory data cannot settle. For each: what the thesis says, why the laboratory pattern cannot answer, which probe can (with the pattern change computed on the contrast page), on which samples, what result to expect, and why it matters for the electrolyte. Priority 1 questions are the spine of the first proposals; priority 3 waits for a second round.

G01priority 1

Where gallium sits, and how acentric the garnet is

What the thesis says

The cubic garnet is refined in \(I\bar{4}3d\) for every Ga–M–Ta composition and for the Ga–Y–Sc series, "commonly adopted for Ga-substituted LLZO" (§III.3.1, p. 59; §IV.3.1, p. 86). Only the lattice parameter and the phase fractions are reported; no atomic coordinate, site occupancy or displacement parameter is given anywhere (Tables III-S1, IV-1).

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

The space group is acentric because the lithium sublattice and the oxygen framework lose the inversion of \(Ia\bar{3}d\): 12a and 12b are inversion images of each other. Putting Ga on one or the other is therefore meaningful only relative to that distortion, and a laboratory pattern dominated by La and Zr (89 % of the X-ray scattering power) hardly sees it: the acentric shift of the oxygens changes the Cu Kα pattern by 0.09 %, the neutron pattern by 0.88 %.

What the literature already has

Neutrons and single crystals put Ga on 12a in \(I\bar{4}3d\) above about 0.07–0.15 Ga per formula unit (Wagner et al. 2016, SPODI; Rettenwander et al. 2016, ILL D20); Fe does the same. But at the thesis's very composition, \(\mathrm{Ga_{0.2}Li_{6.3}La_3Zr_2O_{12}}\), synchrotron diffraction with high-field NMR finds Ga only on 24d of \(Ia\bar{3}d\) and assigns the other Ga signals to \(\mathrm{LiGaO_{2}}\) (Vema et al. 2023), and the Ga–Ta neutron study closest to the thesis refines \(Ia\bar{3}d\) (Christopher et al. 2023, ECHIDNA). At 0.2 Ga the space group is contested in the literature.

What can

Neutron powder diffraction at high resolution (ILL D2B), refined jointly with synchrotron data (ESRF ID22) in diff-ref-struct: O1 and O2 refined independently, Ga on 12a with Li on 12a, 12b and 48e.

Samples

The two references (Ga–Ta, Ga–Sc) and the key set K12.

Expected result

The acentric distortion as a number per composition (the O1–O2 splitting, the 12a/12b Li–O distances) and the Ga occupancy of 12a with its esd; whether the distortion changes with the La-site substituent and between the Ta and Sc families.

Why it matters

The thesis explains the high conductivity of Ga-LLZO by the \(I\bar{4}3d\) Li pathways (§I.2.3). Whether the tri-doped garnets keep, lose or strengthen that distortion is the structural fact behind the claim.

G02priority 1

How much lithium the garnets contain, and where

What the thesis says

The lithium vacancy concentration is the thesis's main explanatory variable: it decides the cubic–tetragonal balance ("about 0.7 vacancies per formula unit, above the critical value of approximately 0.4 to 0.5", p. 63), the Rb series's tetragonal fraction and conductivity loss (p. 76), and the Y-series improvement (p. 76). Lithium is never measured: 15 wt% \(\mathrm{Li_{2}CO_{3}}\) excess is weighed (p. 52), the pellets are sintered 6 h at 1200 °C (p. 55), and all Li contents are nominal.

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

Lithium carries 0.4 % of the X-ray scattering power of the garnet. Adding 0.3 Li per formula unit changes the Cu Kα pattern by 0.52 %, about what counting statistics and a background choice change; the neutron pattern by 1.42 %, with lithium scattering in antiphase (b = −1.90 fm) to every other atom.

What the literature already has

Li occupancies of doped LLZO have been refined from natural-Li neutron data many times (Xie et al. 2011; Thompson et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2015; Christopher et al. 2023, where Li falls from 6.66 to 4.94 per formula unit with Ga in Ga–Ta LLZO made with the same 15 % Li excess as the thesis). Meesala et al. 2019 found the tetrahedral Li occupancy rising with the number of dopants in Ta–Ba–Ga LLZO. Never for a systematic matrix at fixed processing.

What can

Neutron powder diffraction (D2B at room temperature; D20 for the whole matrix), with the lithium content refined over 12a, 12b and 48e, constrained by ICP-OES Li analysis of the same powder.

Samples

K12 on D2B; all 38 on D20.

Expected result

Refined Li per formula unit and its distribution over the tetrahedral and octahedral sites, with esd, for each composition; the measured vacancy concentration set against the nominal one (0.7 for Ga–Ta, 0.5 for Ga–Sc, reduced by Ba and Rb), against the tetragonal fraction, and against \(\sigma_\mathrm{RT}\) and \(E_\mathrm{a}\).

Why it matters

It turns the thesis's central argument (vacancies set phase and conductivity) from inference into measurement, composition by composition, and gives the ML chapter the structural descriptors its Perspectives ask for (p. 126).

G03priority 1

Aluminium from the crucibles

What the thesis says

Pellets are sintered at 1200 °C for 6 h, buried in mother powder in covered alumina boats (p. 55). Aluminium is not analysed and not mentioned as a possible dopant.

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

Al\(^{3}\)⁺ from alumina is a well known involuntary dopant of LLZO and goes to the same tetrahedral Li sites as Ga\(^{3}\)⁺. In X-rays it is a light atom beside La; in neutrons its scattering length (3.45 fm) differs from both Li (−1.90 fm) and Ga (7.29 fm). 0.1 Al per formula unit changes the Cu Kα pattern by 0.71 %, the neutron pattern by 1.39 %.

What the literature already has

Al from crucibles was what stabilised the first cubic LLZO (Geiger et al. 2011); some groups avoid it with zirconia or MgO crucibles (Christopher et al. 2023). It is assumed far more often than it is measured.

What can

ICP-OES of sintered pellets (Al, Ga, Li), and neutron diffraction of crushed pellets with Ga and Al refined on 12a under the ICP constraint.

Samples

Sintered pellets of the references and of the best compositions (Ga–Y₀.₂₀–Ta, Ga–Rb₀.₀₃–Sc).

Expected result

The Al content of the conductivity samples, or an upper bound; if Al is present, which site it shares with Ga.

Why it matters

Every conductivity in the thesis was measured on sintered pellets. If they contain Al, the best compositions are Ga–Al–M–Ta and Ga–Al–M–Sc, and the comparison between series must include it.

G04priority 1

Where yttrium sits: the La site or the Zr site

What the thesis says

The slight lattice expansion of the Ga–Y–Ta series (\(12.983 \to 12.994\) Å) is read as "part of the Y\(^{3}\)⁺ occupies the octahedral Zr site", supported by the rise of \(\mathrm{Li_{2}ZrO_{3}}\) (p. 63); the same partial occupation is then used to explain the tetragonal fraction (p. 64), the extra lithium and the best conductivity of the thesis, 1.04 mS \(\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\) at x = 0.20 (p. 76). In the Ga–Y–Sc series the opposite is concluded: the lattice contracts and Y is on the La site (p. 89).

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

Y and Zr differ by one electron (Z = 39, 40): no X-ray pattern can tell them apart on the octahedral site (0.06 % change for 0.1 Y per formula unit). Neutrons cannot either: b(Y) = 7.75 fm against b(Zr) = 7.16 fm gives 0.16 %. This question is not for neutrons. What X-rays can measure is the La site: 0.1 Y on the La site changes the pattern by 1.8 % (Z = 39 against 57), so a precise La-site occupancy, with the Y content fixed by ICP, says how much Y is not there.

What the literature already has

Y on the Zr site of LLZO has been argued only from a slight lattice expansion (Hitz et al. 2013), the thesis's own argument. No Y K-edge study of a garnet electrolyte was found; recent neutron studies of Y-containing medium-entropy garnets do not report the Y site.

What can

Y K-edge EXAFS (17.04 keV; ESRF BM23, or BM25 SpLine through the Spanish route): coordination 6 on the Zr site, 8 on the La site, Y–O near 2.2 Å against 2.4 Å. La-site occupancy from high-resolution synchrotron diffraction (ID22). Resonant powder diffraction at the Y K edge (ID22) as a direct, site-resolved contrast.

Samples

Ga–Y–Ta at x = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30; Ga–Y–Sc at 0.05 and 0.20; \(\mathrm{Y_{2}O_{3}}\), YSZ and a Y-garnet as references.

Expected result

The fraction of Y on each site for each composition, and whether it differs between the Ta and Sc families as the thesis's two readings imply.

Why it matters

The best composition of the thesis rests its explanation on this site. Measured, it either becomes the thesis's strongest structural result or is replaced by a better one.

G05priority 2

Is it \(I\bar{4}3d\) everywhere, and where does the tetragonal phase start

What the thesis says

\(I\bar{4}3d\) "maintained throughout" (General Conclusions, p. 123), while tetragonal LLZO is refined at 3–21 wt% in the Rb and Y series (Table III-S1) and the Ga–Sc reference is described as half tetragonal (p. 88) but refined as single cubic (Fig. IV-2a).

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

The weak reflections that distinguish \(I\bar{4}3d\) from \(Ia\bar{3}d\), like (310), and the small splittings of the tetragonal cell are at the limit of laboratory resolution and counting statistics. Their intensity comes mostly from the lithium sublattice and the oxygen distortion, where neutrons are 3–9 times more sensitive.

What the literature already has

Two causes can make a garnet \(I\bar{4}3d\): Ga (or Fe) ordering on 12a, and protonation, which neutrons showed to give an acentric \(I\bar{4}3d\) phase with H on the octahedral sites (Orera et al. 2016, Spanish neutron group). No study has separated the two in a Ga-doped garnet; aged powders cannot be read without it (G08).

What can

High-resolution synchrotron diffraction (ID22, instrumental FWHM 0.0025°) for the splittings and the weak reflections of all 38 powders; neutrons (D2B, D20) for the intensities; refinement in both space groups compared on the same data.

Samples

All 38 on ID22; K12 on D2B.

Expected result

For each composition: the space group the data support, the tetragonal fraction with esd and its detection limit (\(\approx 0.1\) wt% on ID22), the tetragonality c/a of the minor phase.

Why it matters

It fixes the phase state that every conductivity is compared against, and settles the Chapter IV baseline.

G06priority 2

How much Ba and Rb the garnet holds

What the thesis says

Ba: lattice parameter nearly constant (12.969–12.981 Å) although Ba\(^{2}\)⁺ is larger than La\(^{3}\)⁺, read as limited incorporation; \(\mathrm{BaZrO_{3}}\) from x = 0.15 (p. 62). Rb: steady expansion to 13.001 Å, read as progressive incorporation (p. 62).

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

Ba (Z = 56) and La (Z = 57) are indistinguishable by X-rays: 0.1 Ba per formula unit on the La site changes the Cu Kα pattern by 0.09 %. Neutrons separate them well, b(Ba) = 5.07 fm against b(La) = 8.24 fm: 1.0 % change, twelve times more. For Rb the roles reverse: X-rays see it (Z = 37, 1.9 %) better than neutrons (0.37 %).

What the literature already has

Ba with Ta has been reported single cubic up to 0.30 Ba (Inada et al. 2016), against the thesis's limit near 0.10; with Ga, \(\mathrm{BaZrO_{3}}\) forms between grains (Tian et al. 2022). Rb on the La site of Ga-LLZO is published at 0.05 only (Wu et al. 2017); the thesis goes to 0.30, six times higher.

What can

Neutrons (D2B) for the La-site occupancy of the Ba series; synchrotron (ID22) for the Rb series; Rb K-edge EXAFS (15.2 keV, BM23) for the local environment; the phase fraction of \(\mathrm{BaZrO_{3}}\) from ID22.

Samples

Ga–Ba–Ta at x = 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30; Ga–Rb–Ta at 0.05, 0.20; Ga–Rb–Sc at 0.03, 0.20.

Expected result

The Ba solubility limit measured in the garnet (not inferred from \(\mathrm{BaZrO_{3}}\)), the Rb content of the garnet, and the lithium that compensates each.

Why it matters

The thesis ranks the three substituents by how much lithium each adds; that ranking needs the amount of each that is actually in the garnet.

G07priority 2

Ta and Sc on the Zr site: the two families on one footing

What the thesis says

Chapter IV was not re-refined with the Chapter III protocol: its lattice parameters (13.03–13.07 Å, two decimals, Table IV-1) are 0.06–0.09 Å above Chapter III's for an almost identical framework; \(\mathrm{Sc_{2}O_{3}}\) appears in the Rb series and \(\mathrm{Y_{2}O_{3}}\) in the Y series; Sc is assumed on the Zr site (p. 88).

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

The two families need the same data and the same refinement to be compared. Sc on the Zr site is seen by both probes (X-rays 3.8 %, neutrons 1.4 %, with b(Sc) = 12.29 fm the strongest scatterer of the cell); Ta by X-rays only (Z = 73; neutrons 0.03 %). Sc\(^{3}\)⁺ could also go to a Li site; the scattering length tells it apart from Li and Ga.

What the literature already has

The direct precedent of the Sc family, Ga on Li with Sc on Zr (Buannic et al. 2017, 1.8 mS \(\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\)), used DFT, XRD, ICP, NMR and STEM, no neutrons. Sc in LLZO has never been refined from neutron data, although its contrast with Zr is the strongest of the cell.

What can

All 18 Ga–M–Sc powders on ID22 with the Ga–M–Ta set, refined with one protocol; the Sc compositions of K12 on D2B; Sc K-edge EXAFS at BM08 LISA or BM20 (4.49 keV, below BM23's range).

Samples

All 38 (ID22); Ga–Sc, Ga–Rb₀.₀₃–Sc, Ga–Rb₀.₂₀–Sc, Ga–Y₀.₁₀–Sc (D2B).

Expected result

One table of lattice parameters with esd for the 38 compositions, from one instrument and one protocol; the site of Sc.

Why it matters

The thesis's cross-family conclusion (the inversion of the Rb and Y rankings between families, p. 123) depends on it.

G08priority 2

Protons: what air has done to the powders

What the thesis says

Not discussed for the bulk. Oxygen enrichment at grain boundaries is seen by EDS and attributed to \(\mathrm{Li_{2}CO_{3}}\) formed on exposure of fractured surfaces to air (p. 69).

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

Li⁺/H⁺ exchange in moist air is well documented for garnets and changes the lattice parameter and the Li content. X-rays do not see hydrogen; neutrons see it twice, by its negative scattering length on the Li sites and by its incoherent scattering (80 b) in the background. 0.5 H⁺ for 0.5 Li⁺ changes the Cu Kα pattern by 0.59 %, the neutron pattern by 2.3 %.

What the literature already has

Exchange starts on the first exposure to air and Ga-LLZO behaves like Al-LLZO (Redhammer et al. 2021); octahedral 48e Li goes first (Redhammer et al. 2022); deep hydration gives an \(I\bar{4}3d\) phase (Orera et al. 2016); dry sintering avoids it (Bernuy-López et al. 2014).

What can

Neutron background and Li-site refinement (D2B, D20); TGA–MS of the same powders; fresh samples kept under argon from synthesis to beam.

Samples

Old and fresh powder of the same composition (Ga–Ta, Ga–Y₀.₂₀–Ta); a deliberately aged sample.

Expected result

The H content of the samples as kept, the rate of exchange, and the protocol that the beam-time samples must follow.

Why it matters

Without it, every Li occupancy of G02 is uncertain by the unknown protonation.

G09priority 1

Phase fractions that close: \(\mathrm{Li_{2}ZrO_{3}}\), and powders against pellets

What the thesis says

14.2 wt% \(\mathrm{Li_{2}ZrO_{3}}\) in the best composition (Ga–Y₀.₂₀–Ta), explained by Zr displaced by Y (p. 63) and said not to limit transport (p. 76). All phase fractions come from calcined powders (900 °C, p. 59); all conductivities from pellets sintered at 1200 °C (p. 55).

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

14.24 wt% \(\mathrm{Li_{2}ZrO_{3}}\) beside 85.76 wt% garnet is 0.92 \(\mathrm{Li_{2}ZrO_{3}}\) per garnet formula unit (fact base), while Y on the Zr site can displace at most x = 0.20 Zr. The mass balance does not close: either the light monoclinic minor phase, with heavily overlapping reflections, is overestimated on laboratory data, or Zr leaves the garnet for another reason and the garnet is Zr-poor. The phase content of the pellets, the samples of every conductivity, was not measured.

What the literature already has

Excess lithium pushes Ga out of the garnet into \(\mathrm{LiGaO_{2}}\) (Vema et al. 2023), and Ga-rich compositions give \(\mathrm{La_{2}Zr_{2}O_{7}}\) + \(\mathrm{LiGaO_{2}}\) (Rettenwander et al. 2014): with 15 % \(\mathrm{Li_{2}CO_{3}}\) excess, how much of the nominal 0.2 Ga is in the garnet is itself a question. In situ neutron studies show LLZO forming from a carbonate melt and residual phases left by \(\mathrm{Li_{2}CO_{3}}\) evaporation (Rao et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2015).

What can

High-resolution synchrotron diffraction (ID22) of powders and of crushed pellets of the same compositions, with an internal standard for the amorphous fraction; neutron diffraction, where \(\mathrm{Li_{2}ZrO_{3}}\) scatters differently from the garnet; ICP for the Zr/La ratio.

Samples

All 38 powders and 12 sintered pellets (crushed) on ID22.

Expected result

Phase fractions with esd and detection limits for powders and pellets, \(\mathrm{LiGaO_{2}}\) and \(\mathrm{La_{2}Zr_{2}O_{7}}\) looked for explicitly; how much of the nominal Ga is in the garnet; a closed mass balance (Li, Zr, La) for each composition; the evolution of the phase state from 900 °C to 1200 °C.

Why it matters

The conductivities can be interpreted only against the phase content of the pellets they were measured on.

G10priority 3

Lithium pathways and their temperature dependence

What the thesis says

Activation energies from 0.32 to 0.46 eV, total (bulk and grain boundary unresolved) for all Ta compositions (Table III-2); the decrease to 0.32 eV for Ga–Y₀.₂₀–Ta is read as "a more favorable Li⁺ migration environment within the lattice" (p. 77).

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

Lithium transport pathways show up as anharmonic, elongated nuclear density between 12a/12b and 48e, which neutron diffraction at high temperature maps (Fourier and maximum-entropy maps, anharmonic displacement parameters). X-rays cannot see it.

What the literature already has

High-temperature neutron diffraction with maximum-entropy maps showed the Li pathway in LLZO (Han et al. 2012, ⁷Li sample, LANSCE); single-crystal neutron diffraction of \(\mathrm{Li_{6}La_{3}ZrTaO_{12}}\) to 873 K resolved the Li1 displacement towards 96h (Redhammer et al. 2021).

What can

Neutron diffraction from room temperature to 900–1000 K (D2B furnace for high-Q data; D20 for continuous ramps and the tetragonal–cubic transition of the Rb and Y series).

Samples

Ga–Ta, Ga–Y₀.₂₀–Ta, Ga–Rb₀.₀₃–Sc, Ga–Ba₀.₁₀–Ta.

Expected result

Li nuclear density maps at several temperatures; the bottleneck of the 12a/12b–48e path; whether the lowest-\(E_\mathrm{a}\) composition has the most open path; the tetragonal–cubic transition temperatures.

Why it matters

It links the measured activation energies to the structure, which is what the thesis's Chapter III conclusion claims and cannot show.

G11priority 3

Local order: dopant clusters and lithium short-range order

What the thesis says

EDS maps at the micrometre scale show homogeneous Ga, Y and Rb (pp. 69, 91); local structure is not addressed.

Why the laboratory pattern cannot settle it

Average-structure refinement cannot see Ga–Li or dopant–vacancy short-range order, nor the local distortion around Y or Rb. Total scattering (PDF) can, with X-rays for the heavy-atom framework and neutrons for Li–O and Li–Li.

What the literature already has

The only total-scattering study of cubic LLZO is of the undoped compound (Tian et al. 2023, neutron and X-ray PDF with reverse Monte Carlo).

What can

X-ray PDF (ESRF ID15A or ID22 fast-PDF), neutron PDF (ILL D4c).

Samples

Ga–Ta, Ga–Y₀.₂₀–Ta, Ga–Rb₀.₀₃–Sc.

Expected result

Local distortions around the substituents; Li–Li correlations; the length scale over which the average \(I\bar{4}3d\) structure holds.

Why it matters

Second round: it explains what the average structure of G01–G02 will leave unexplained.
